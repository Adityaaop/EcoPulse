Medical Triage

Smog & Fog Acute Symptom Countermeasures

Immediate first-aid steps if you experience acute distress after exposure to toxic aerosol smog or heavy cold fog.

Burning / Red Eyes

Caused by acidic nitric/sulfuric micro-condensates and peroxyacetyl nitrate (PAN).

  • Flush eyes with sterile lubricating saline or preservative-free artificial tears.
  • Do NOT rub eyes (prevents abrasive corneal micro-lacerations from soot).
  • Apply a cool, clean damp compress over closed eyelids for 10 minutes.

Dry Throat & Persistent Cough

Irritation of laryngeal cilia and upper mucosal membranes by coarse and fine particulates.

  • Gargle with lukewarm saline water (1/2 tsp salt in warm water).
  • Perform steam inhalation with 2-3 drops of eucalyptus oil for 5 minutes.
  • Sip warm honey and ginger tea to stimulate protective mucus hydration.

Wheezing / Chest Tightness

Acute bronchospasm and alveolar inflammation triggered by deep PM2.5 infiltration.

  • Asthma/COPD patients: Administer 2 puffs of rescue bronchodilator (Albuterol) immediately via spacer.
  • Sit upright in a HEPA-purified sealed room; do not lie flat.
  • If breathing difficulty persists beyond 15 minutes, proceed to emergency medical care.

Headache & Heavy Fatigue

Systemic hypoxemia and elevated carbon monoxide / volatile chemical absorption in traffic.

  • Move to high-ACH filtered indoor air immediately.
  • Hydrate abundantly to support cellular renal clearance of absorbed chemical metabolites.
  • Avoid caffeine or energy drinks that can further elevate cardiac strain.
